Section 5: Academic Concepts & Research Language

37. Collocation: Research Leadership
"The principal ________ (investigator / participant / novelty) designed the double-blind experiment to test false memory formation."
investigator
Academic Context: "Principal investigator" is the formal designation for the lead researcher directing an experiment or laboratory grant project.
38. Academic Dependent Preposition
"Elderly patients who suffer from chronic fatigue may be more ________ (prone to / familiar with / consistent in) confusing prescription instructions."
prone to
Academic Context: "Prone to" denotes an inclination or vulnerability to an adverse condition (e.g., prone to false memories or errors).
39. Reading Strategy: NO vs. NOT GIVEN
Explain the difference between an answer evaluated as NO and one marked NOT GIVEN in IELTS Reading.
  • NO: The passage explicitly contradicts the writer's claim or argument.
  • NOT GIVEN: The passage lacks sufficient details to know what the author thinks about the statement.
40. Cognitive Science: Source Memory
In memory research, what is "source memory," and why is it considered a vital element of accurate recall?
Source memory refers to the context in which information was acquired (e.g., where, when, or from whom it was seen or heard). It is vital because remembering the origin enables the brain to distinguish true facts from incorrect or suggested details.
41. Life Patterns & Novelty
Why do individuals tend to seek less novelty and become more conventional as they grow into middle age?
Adults must manage routine responsibilities (careers, raising children, elder care) that demand consistency. Unfamiliar experiences and sudden changes bring insecurity, leading most people to prefer the familiar.
42. Dependent Preposition Collocation
"Younger adults generally show a greater interest ________ novelty than older generations."
in ("an interest in something").

Section 6: Speaking Part 3 – Generalising & Pronunciation

Adopt broad sociological perspectives and master clause intonation patterns.

43. Analysis of Candidate Responses
Why is the answer "I always keep a small notepad in my pocket" rated weak for the Part 3 question: "How do people ensure they do not forget daily tasks?"
Speaking Part 3 requires discussing people and society in general. Answering with a personal habit ("I...") is too narrow, overly short, and provides only one isolated technique without general elaboration.
44. Sentence Generalisation Transformation
Rewrite this personal statement using generalising markers (e.g., many people, tend to, individuals):
"I always forget my doctor's appointment if I don't write it on my hand."
Model Response: "Many people tend to forget important appointments unless they write reminders on their hands, record dates in a diary, or set digital calendar alarms."
45. Intonation Markers for Lists
Determine whether the voice pitch should RISE or FALL on each bracketed item:
"To remember daily chores, people often consult a diary [1], check a mobile calendar [2], or make a written list [3]."
  • [1] diary: ↗ RISE (shows the list is continuing)
  • [2] calendar: ↗ RISE (shows the list is continuing)
  • [3] list: ↘ FALL (signals final item / completion of thought)
46. Intonation in Conditional Clauses
What communicative signal does a rising pitch send when pausing at the end of an introductory condition clause (e.g., "If someone is under severe pressure at work... [↗]")?
It tells the listener that the speaker has not finished what they are saying and that the main consequence is about to follow.

Complete this First Conditional sentence to argue that talent alone is insufficient:

Model: "...she practises hard and regularly, works closely with a coach, and maintains absolute dedication."
49. Collocation: "contribute to success"
Write a supporting sentence incorporating additional factors (luck, money, networking), using the phrase contribute to success.
Model: "Furthermore, external circumstances such as financial security and meeting the right mentors also contribute significantly to success."
50. Essential Elements of a Task 2 Conclusion
What two elements must be included when writing an essay conclusion beginning with "In conclusion"?
  1. Summary of Main Points: A concise synthesis of both sides discussed in the body paragraphs.
  2. Clear Personal Verdict: An unambiguous statement confirming the writer's final opinion.
